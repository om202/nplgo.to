## QR Codes in Nepal: From Curiosity to Critical Infrastructure

Five years ago, QR codes in Nepal were novelties. Today, they are infrastructure.

According to Nepal Rastra Bank data, QR-based payments in Nepal surged from NPR 20.28 billion in FY 2020/21 to NPR 958.38 billion in FY 2024/25, a nearly 50x increase. By early 2026, monthly QR transaction volumes exceed 33 million, with a value of over NPR 96 billion per month. The number of merchants accepting QR payments has grown from 282,000 in mid-2021 to 2.34 million by January 2025.

This is not just a payments story. QR codes have fundamentally changed how Nepali businesses connect the physical and digital worlds: restaurant menus, product packaging, event check-ins, tourist information, real estate listings, and marketing campaigns all depend on QR codes.

This guide is written for Nepali businesses, marketers, and creators who want to use QR codes effectively, covering everything from the Nepal-specific payment ecosystem to the exact technical specifications that determine whether your printed QR code actually scans.

## Nepal's QR Payment Ecosystem: Understanding the Landscape

Before diving into marketing applications, it's important to understand the payment QR ecosystem, because most Nepali consumers' first exposure to QR codes is through payments.

### Key Platforms

Nepal's digital payment landscape is dominated by three interconnected systems:

- **eSewa**: Nepal's largest digital wallet, with a massive merchant and user network
- **IME Khalti**: Formed from the 2025 merger of Khalti and IME Pay, consolidating two major wallets
- **Fonepay**: Nepal's largest payment network, connecting banks and wallets through a unified QR system

### Cross-Border QR Payments

One of the most significant developments in 2025-2026 is the Fonepay-UPI cross-border integration. Since March 2024, Indian tourists can scan Fonepay QR codes at Nepali merchants and pay using UPI-enabled Indian apps. By July 2025, over 1 million cross-border UPI transactions had been processed in Nepal, valued at over NPR 2 billion.

This matters for tourism-focused businesses: if you run a hotel, restaurant, or shop in Kathmandu, Pokhara, or Lumbini, Indian tourists can already pay you through your existing Fonepay QR setup without needing cash exchange.

Nepal Rastra Bank has also authorized Nepali citizens to use QR code payment systems in India and Bhutan, with a daily limit of INR 15,000 and a monthly limit of INR 100,000, though the reciprocal service is still pending full rollout.

## Marketing QR Codes vs Payment QR Codes

This distinction is critical and often misunderstood.

**Payment QR codes** are generated by eSewa, Khalti, Fonepay, or your bank. They are linked to your merchant account and trigger financial transactions when scanned. You cannot customize these or track marketing metrics through them.

**Marketing QR codes** link to URLs: your website, digital menu, social media profile, event registration page, or any web page. These are the QR codes you create with tools like [npgo.to](/) and they serve a fundamentally different purpose: driving traffic, sharing information, and tracking engagement.

Most businesses need both. A restaurant might have a Fonepay QR sticker at the counter for payments and a separate npgo.to QR code on each table linking to the digital menu.

## Technical Guide: Printing QR Codes That Actually Work

This section covers the specifications that determine whether your printed QR code is scannable. Getting these wrong means wasted print runs and frustrated customers.

### Minimum Size

- **Close range** (business cards, flyers, product labels): minimum 2x2 cm. For business cards, use 3 cm for reliability.
- **Medium range** (table tents, counter displays): 4-6 cm works well.
- **Distance scanning** (posters, banners, signage): use the 10:1 rule. The QR code should be at least 1/10th of the scanning distance. A poster scanned from 1 meter away needs a 10 cm QR code. A banner scanned from 3 meters needs a 30 cm QR code.

### Resolution and DPI

- **300 DPI** for all close-range print: business cards, flyers, brochures, packaging, table tents. This is the standard for commercial printing.
- **150 DPI** is acceptable for large-format prints: banners, billboards, trade show backdrops, where scanning distance is several meters.
- npgo.to generates QR codes at 2000x2000 pixels, which provides enough resolution for prints up to approximately 17 cm (6.7 inches) at 300 DPI.

### File Format

- **SVG or EPS**: vector formats, infinitely scalable without quality loss. Ideal for professional print shops.
- **PNG**: raster format, good for most applications if the resolution is high enough. npgo.to provides high-resolution PNG.
- **Never use JPEG** for QR codes. JPEG compression creates artifacts that blur the sharp edges between modules, reducing scannability.

### Contrast and Color

- Maintain a minimum **4:1 contrast ratio** between the QR code modules (dark squares) and the background.
- **Dark modules on a light background** is the standard. This works with virtually all scanners.
- Avoid inverted colors (light code on dark background). While some modern scanners handle this, it can reduce scan reliability by up to 40% on older devices.
- Avoid placing QR codes on busy patterns, textured surfaces, or reflective materials.

### The Quiet Zone

Every QR code needs a clear border of empty space around it called the "quiet zone." This should be at least 4 modules wide on all sides (roughly 2-3 mm for a standard-sized code). Without it, scanners cannot reliably detect the code's boundaries.

Do not crop QR codes tightly. Do not overlap text, logos, or decorative elements with the quiet zone.

### Error Correction Levels

QR codes have built-in redundancy that allows them to be scanned even if partially damaged:

| Level | Data Recovery | Best For |
|-------|--------------|----------|
| L (Low) | ~7% | Clean, controlled environments, digital displays |
| M (Medium) | ~15% | Standard marketing materials, flyers, posters |
| Q (Quartile) | ~25% | Outdoor use, minor weather exposure, small logo overlays |
| H (High) | ~30% | Heavy branding with logo in center, extreme conditions |

If you plan to add a logo to the center of your QR code, always use Level Q or H to ensure the code remains scannable.

## 7 Common QR Code Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Printing too small**: A QR code under 2 cm will fail to scan for many users, especially in poor lighting. When in doubt, go bigger.

2. **Low contrast colors**: Gray on white, navy on black, or colored codes on colored backgrounds. Always maintain a 4:1 contrast ratio.

3. **Using JPEG format**: JPEG compression blurs the sharp edges QR scanners depend on. Use PNG or SVG.

4. **No quiet zone**: Placing text, borders, or graphics right up against the QR code. Leave at least 4 modules of empty space on all sides.

5. **Linking to non-mobile-optimized pages**: 82.8% of Nepal's mobile connections are broadband-enabled and most QR scans happen on phones. If your landing page isn't mobile-friendly, you're wasting the scan.

6. **Not testing before printing**: Always test your QR code on at least 3 different phones, in different lighting conditions, and at the intended scanning distance before committing to a print run.

7. **Using overly long URLs**: Long URLs create denser, more complex QR patterns that are harder to scan. Always shorten your URL first with [npgo.to](/), then generate the QR code. Shorter URLs produce cleaner, more reliable QR codes.
